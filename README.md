# Boardroom to Buildroom

**A role-based exploration of operational AI governance, accountability, observability, and evidence.**

Boardroom to Buildroom examines a practical question that is often lost between AI principles, policy, regulation, and implementation:

> What does responsible AI governance actually require from the person sitting in each seat — from the boardroom to the people building, operating, and relying on AI systems?

This repository supports a public research and writing series. It is intentionally being developed in stages and pressure-tested against practitioner feedback before being presented as a mature operating model.

## Current status

**Status:** Early-stage research and public working framework  
**Initial focus:** Enterprise / corporate AI governance  
**Later areas:** Startups, research, education, and selected high-consequence sectors after appropriate domain research  
**Not currently positioned as:** a legal standard, certification scheme, complete compliance program, or substitute for organization-specific professional advice

The first publishing season is designed to test a set of governance problems before the formal role-based playbooks are developed.

## Season 1: Thesis and pressure-testing

1. AI governance has an accountability problem
2. Human-in-the-loop isn't necessarily human oversight
3. Who owns an AI decision?
4. The AI system you approved may no longer exist — Governance Decay
5. AI governance needs observability
6. What should a Board actually ask about AI?
7. What can a CEO never delegate about AI governance?
8. Why “Responsible AI owns it” is dangerous
9. The middle-manager AI governance problem
10. What should an engineer do when responsibility exceeds authority?

These posts are not ten versions of “accountability matters.” Each tests a different failure mode: fragmentation, oversight capacity, ownership taxonomy, material change, runtime evidence, board visibility, executive decision authority, governance-function boundaries, management translation, and escalation when authority is insufficient.

## Working backbone

The current working backbone is:

**KNOW → OWN → BOUND → CONTROL → OBSERVE → INTERVENE → ESCALATE → PROVE → IMPROVE**

This is a proposed organizing model for this project. It is not represented as an established industry standard.

## Evidence labels

Material in this repository should be distinguishable as one of the following:

- **Established** — directly supported by applicable law, regulation, recognized standard/framework, or authoritative primary source.
- **Interpretation** — an analysis or conclusion drawn from established material and identified as such.
- **Proposed** — a concept, taxonomy, model, term, or practice introduced by this project.
- **Open question** — an issue intentionally left for practitioner challenge, research, or refinement.

The project does not convert recommendations from voluntary frameworks into legal obligations unless an applicable legal source independently creates that obligation.

## Publication standard

Every public artifact derived from this repository must pass:

1. Substance and specificity review
2. Evidence and terminology verification
3. Liability / Provenance Check
4. Human-authorship editorial review
5. Factual-integrity review
6. Final source and date check for time-sensitive claims

The Liability / Provenance Check is mandatory. See [METHODOLOGY.md](METHODOLOGY.md).

## Feedback and corrections

The first 10–15 public pieces are treated as research as much as publication. Material practitioner disagreement may change the framework. Substantive changes should be recorded in [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md), with source corrections reflected in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md) and terminology changes reflected in [TERMINOLOGY.md](TERMINOLOGY.md).
