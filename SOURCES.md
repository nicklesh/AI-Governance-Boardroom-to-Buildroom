# Sources

**Purpose:** Master evidence register for Boardroom to Buildroom.  
**Last reviewed:** August 11, 2026

This file is intentionally selective. A source is included because it is expected to support, constrain, or challenge the framework — not merely because it discusses AI governance.

## Source-status key

- **Primary / official** — issuing authority or official publisher
- **Binding law** — legal text whose applicability depends on jurisdiction, scope, classification, and effective date
- **Voluntary framework / standard** — not automatically a legal obligation
- **Guidance / principles** — authoritative but not necessarily legally binding

## Foundational sources

### NIST AI Risk Management Framework

**Publisher:** U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)  
**Status:** Primary / official; voluntary framework  
**Current reference:** AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF)  
**URL:** https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework

**Why it matters:** NIST states that the AI RMF is intended for voluntary use and is designed to help organizations incorporate trustworthiness considerations into the design, development, use, and evaluation of AI systems.

**Likely Season 1 relevance:** governance across lifecycle; organizational roles; risk mapping; measurement; governance functions; generative-AI profile.

**Editorial caution:** Do not convert NIST recommendations into legal requirements unless another applicable authority independently does so.

### ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — Artificial intelligence management system

**Publisher:** International Organization for Standardization / IEC  
**Status:** International management-system standard; adoption or contractual/regulatory relevance depends on context  
**Official overview:** https://www.iso.org/standard/42001

**Why it matters:** Provides an organization-wide management-system structure for AI governance and risk/opportunity management, including continual improvement.

**Likely Season 1 relevance:** governance operating systems, roles, lifecycle management, continual improvement, management accountability.

**Editorial caution:** ISO standards are copyrighted. Summarize and cite; do not reproduce protected control text, tables, figures, or substantial excerpts without appropriate rights.

---

### ISO/IEC 23894:2023 — AI risk-management guidance

**Publisher:** ISO / IEC  
**Status:** International guidance standard  
**Official overview:** https://www.iso.org/standard/77304.html

**Why it matters:** Provides AI-specific risk-management guidance and useful terminology against which proposed project concepts should be tested.

---

### EU Artificial Intelligence Act — Regulation (EU) 2024/1689

**Publisher:** European Union  
**Status:** Binding law within its scope; obligations apply according to the Regulation's scope and phased application dates  
**European Commission overview:** https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai

**Why it matters:** Risk-based legal framework with obligations that differ across prohibited practices, high-risk systems, transparency contexts, general-purpose AI, and other categories.

**Likely Season 1 relevance:** human oversight; risk management; post-market/lifecycle obligations; governance claims involving regulated systems.

**Editorial caution:** Never summarize an AI Act obligation without checking applicability, actor role, system classification, jurisdictional reach, and the relevant application date.

## Evidence-dossier rule

Before a source is used to support a public claim, the research dossier should record:

- exact claim supported;
- source type and authority;
- publication/revision date;
- applicable jurisdiction or scope;
- whether the source is binding, voluntary, or advisory;
- any material limitation or counter-source;
- relevant passage or section location; and
- which Season 1 post(s) rely on it.
